## What 18-1758 lets Omaha do, and what it doesn't

- **It can't ban.** A city may not expressly or effectively prohibit renting a residential property for up to 30 consecutive days.
- **It can regulate for health and safety,** if that's the ordinance's primary purpose. The statute lists fire and building codes, health and sanitation, traffic, solid waste and pollution, and an emergency contact for the property.
- **It can tax.** Subsection 4 allows a sales or occupation tax where other law permits it, and Omaha charges a 5.5% hotel occupation tax.
- **Ordinary rules still apply.** Zoning, noise and nuisance, and property maintenance rules apply to a short-term rental the same as to similar property.

## Covenants are a separate question

Subsection 7 says 18-1758 doesn't affect the regulations of a private entity, including a homeowners association. The Ridges Homeowners Association covers 784 lots across 13 subdivisions between Pacific Street, West Center Road, 180th and 192nd, under covenants first filed in 1993, and Linden Estates, near 144th and Dodge, has a separately managed Second Addition. We haven't read any of these associations' rental rules and don't summarize them here.

## Questions for the City Clerk at 1819 Farnam

1. Has the City Council adopted a short-term rental registration or license ordinance, and from what date?
2. Which department takes registrations, and is there a fee?
3. Does an emergency contact have to be on file, and in what form?
4. Is the property inside Omaha's city limits for the 5.5% occupation tax?
