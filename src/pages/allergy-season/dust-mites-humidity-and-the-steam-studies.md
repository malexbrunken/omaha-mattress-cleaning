## Pollen is outdoors and seasonal; mites are indoors and year-round

| | Pollen | Bed mites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it's counted | Rooftop samplers run by DCHD, UNMC and NAB Station 33 | Not in DCHD's, UNMC's or Station 33's counts |
| Season | March to October in UNMC's calendar | All 12 months, indoors |
| What moves it | Wind: April averages 11.8 mph at Eppley | Humidity in the room |

## Eppley's moisture year, in four numbers

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 hourly normals put Eppley's average dew point at 16°F in January, 61°F in June, 66°F in July and 41°F in October. The dew point measures how much water the outdoor air carries, and when July's 66°F air reaches a cooler bedroom, the room's relative humidity climbs, which is why the EPA's 60 percent line is easiest to cross in an Omaha summer. A hygrometer by the bed tells you more about your room than Eppley's normals can.

## What the two steam studies measured, and what they didn't

- **Glass and Needham (2004):** a steam cleaner at 96°C, applied to carpet and mattress samples, left no surviving Dermatophagoides farinae mites in those samples, a measurement of heat on samples.
- **Colloff and colleagues (1995):** steam-treated carpet squares seeded with mites had no live mites over the following 4 months, which is carpet, not a mattress.

Neither study measured allergy symptoms, an Omaha bedroom or our equipment, so we cite them for the heat effect only; Sleep Sanitation's [steam and bed mite review](https://sleepsanitation.com/knowledge-center/mattress-dust-mites-allergens/does-steam-kill-dust-mites-in-mattresses) walks through both. Killing mites also isn't removing them: the bodies and droppings stay in the mattress until they're vacuumed out, so we HEPA vacuum as well as steam.
