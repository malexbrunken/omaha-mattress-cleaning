## Inside or outside the Omaha city limits

Elkhorn's annexation fight is the reason this question exists. In early 2005 Elkhorn tried to annex surrounding sanitary and improvement districts (SIDs) to grow past 10,000 people, and Omaha passed its own ordinance first. The Nebraska Supreme Court's 2007 opinion describes that race; the result is that the old City of Elkhorn is now Omaha, while land outside the annexed area stayed outside the city.

For a mattress, the split works like this:

- **Inside Omaha city limits.** Your Elkhorn address gets the City of Omaha programs: the free Fall Cleanup Saturdays (October 3, 10, 17 and 24, 2026, 9 AM to 2 PM, one quadrant per date) and the River City bulky program at 6404 S 60th St, where a $15 fee at the first visit of the year buys 4 dump vouchers. Our [Omaha mattress disposal guide](/omaha-homes/getting-rid-of-a-mattress-in-omaha) has the checklist for both.
- **Outside city limits.** The city programs don't apply. Papillion Sanitation, which lists Elkhorn among the areas it serves outside Omaha's limits, takes mattresses on a scheduled bulk pickup for an extra fee, and its residential page says mattresses and box springs must be wrapped in plastic first.

Wasteline's address lookup settles which side you're on. When Papillion Sanitation's own lookup sees an address inside the city, it sends the resident back to Wasteline.

## Elkhorn housing by the numbers

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey puts the median year built for homes in ZIP 68022 at 2011, plus or minus two years. About 52% of its 13,457 housing units were built in 2010 or later and about 7% before 1970. For comparison, the median for the City of Omaha as a whole is 1973, with about 46% of homes built before 1970.

Those figures describe Elkhorn houses, not the beds inside them. A 2016 house can hold a mattress that has moved three times, so the age of the home doesn't change how we clean. The survey also doesn't record basements, so we can't say how many Elkhorn bedrooms are below grade. If yours is, our [Omaha basement humidity guide](/omaha-homes/humid-summers-and-basement-bedrooms) covers what July air does down there.

## If you suspect bed bugs in Elkhorn

Douglas County Health Department's pest page says bed bugs have not been shown to spread disease and that the department's role with them is education. For an Elkhorn home that means the call goes to a licensed pest professional, with the county's Environmental Health line, (402) 444-7485, for questions. We don't treat bed bugs; cleaning comes after treatment, not instead of it. Sleep Sanitation explains how [bed bugs differ from dust mites and bed mites](https://sleepsanitation.com/knowledge-center/mattress-dust-mites-allergens/dust-mites-vs-bed-bugs-vs-bed-mites). This page is general information, not medical advice.

## Finding your way, for orientation only

ZIP 68022 covers Elkhorn on the western edge of Douglas County. Pheasant Point Landfill, the drop-off of last resort in the Omaha guide, is north of Elkhorn at Highway 36 and 216th Street; call (866) 909-4458 before hauling a mattress there, because the city guide says fees may apply.

## An Elkhorn visit

We bring the equipment to the bedroom, and the technician wears gloves and shoe booties. Equipment is disinfected between jobs. After the job we run a moisture test, and we also run a bedroom CO2 test; the UV-C vacuum has a built-in dust mite sensor. Each Elkhorn job is written up on an inspection form covering the mattress material, special care notes and any urine or odor observations. A severe or biohazard case carries a surcharge, and we'll tell you before we start. Check your care label and warranty terms before booking any cleaning.
