## What is the difference between dry vapor steam and wet extraction?

Water is the carrier in wet extraction. A heated solution is sprayed into the fabric and vacuumed back out with what it loosened. That works well on carpet, where the fibers and pad can take it. A mattress is foam, latex or fiber batting under a quilted cover, and some of that water stays in.

Dry vapor steam uses heat as the tool. The vapor is superheated at the nozzle and carries very little liquid water, so it reaches the quilting, seams and tufts without soaking the comfort layers. We follow it with HEPA vacuuming and UV-C light treatment on every visit.

## Why does moisture matter more in an Omaha bedroom?

Omaha's air is most humid in July and August. NOAA's normals put Eppley Airfield's average July dew point near 66°F, against 16°F in January. A basement guest room or a closed-up bedroom in summer gives water in a mattress less chance to leave. Our [humidity and basement bedrooms guide](/omaha-homes/humid-summers-and-basement-bedrooms) has the numbers, and the [Missouri river-bottom humidity guide](/omaha-homes/missouri-river-bottom-humidity) covers east Omaha and Council Bluffs.

## When is wet extraction the right call?

When something has soaked an item and the volume needs flushing out, extraction has a place. A flooded or long-saturated mattress is often past cleaning anyway, and we will tell you if replacing it makes more sense. For routine care, pet odor and ordinary urine accidents, our default is dry vapor steam with enzyme treatment for urine.

## What should you ask any Omaha provider?

- What equipment touches the mattress, and is it the same machine used on the floors?
- How much water goes in, and how do you confirm the mattress is dry to the touch before the bed is made?
- Do you work the seams, tufts and edges, or only the top panel?
- What is included in the price, and what costs extra?
